Tell it your driver, your models and your rates. It tells you the controller.

robot_controllers treats a robot controller as a trajectory plus a feed-forward model plus a feedback model, placed by a layout: where each runs (plan time, the host, or the servo driver), how often, and how far ahead it samples. It grades a population of layouts against the conditions a real arm meets, and answers the question with a lookup.

The idea

What it found

Feedback stays on the driver. At the rig's 50 Hz a driver-closed config beat computed torque on the host on every arm (best Planar twin 1.26 vs 3.13 mm, UR5e 1.65 vs 8.25 mm, BAM five-bar arm 1.5 vs 1.62 mm, 1-DOF pendulum 5.48 vs 5.55 mm). PD on the host saturated the drivers (up to 96.8 % of the time).
Look ahead by the bus delay, and send the secant velocity. The plan is known ahead, so each setpoint is sent early by the time it takes to reach the driver, and the velocity sent is the straight line to the next sample (a moteus walks its setpoint at that velocity between commands). On the UR5e twin that takes the trajectory-forwarding JTC from 6.8 to 1.8 mm at the tool; on the planar twin from 2.78 to 1.36 mm.
Re-send inertia-scaled gains each tick. Re-sending computed-torque gains (diag M(q)·ωn²) with every command, still closed by the driver, was the most accurate on three of four arms: the five-bar goes from 12.1 mm (fixed gains) to 1.5 mm.
Model the friction where it dominates. The five-bar's drives are friction-bound: adding Coulomb friction to the plan-time model takes it from 12.1 to 4.93 mm, where the secant velocity alone did nothing.
A good model notices collisions sooner. With a feed-forward and the feedback on the driver, a hard stop is noticed in 1.25–3.75 ms on every arm; with no feed-forward it takes 4.25–153 ms, and with computed torque on the host 7.25–146 ms. Less torque left for feedback in free motion makes a hit stand out.
Calibrate the delay, but know its limit. One run with no lookahead measures each joint's delay; the friction-aware fit is the one to look ahead by. On a single high-friction joint the joint's own lag swamps the bus (the pendulum reads 19.75 ms on a 1.25 ms bus), so there the datasheet value, or a lookahead sweep on the real arm, is the one to trust.

A UR5e twin on fast moves at 50 Hz, the same law three ways: as built, + lookahead, + secant velocity. The trace is the tool's error.

The scenarios

Around that base, one axis at a time, on fixed target trajectories per arm: trajectory speed (slow, nominal, fast); host rate (25, 50, 100, 200 Hz); how wrong the feed-forward model is (0, 10, 30 %); how soft or stiff the driver's gains are (×0.5, ×1, ×2); the bus (ideal, datasheet, as measured on the rig, with jitter); a collision with a hard stop; and the host rate × model error grid the lookup interpolates over. Metrics: tracking and tool error, saturation, the share of torque feedback supplies, host CPU, bus bytes, impact torque, and time to detect a collision.

Bus timing: the datasheet, a calibration, and a drift alarm

The nominal delay comes from the adapter's and driver's datasheets. One run with no lookahead measures each joint's real delay; a live monitor then watches it and raises an alert when a joint drifts by more than 2 ms. On a slow joint the motor's own friction lag reads as delay, so a calibration reports two numbers: the bus delay (what's compared with the datasheet) and the best lookahead (what the controller uses).

Related work and references

Compliant and impedance controller stacks

  • CRISP controllers · repo · paper
    Torque-based Cartesian impedance and operational-space controllers for ros2_control, with Python and Gymnasium layers, built to track learned policies and teleoperation.
  • ros2_impedance_controller
    Cartesian impedance on ros2_control + Pinocchio: Hogan's law with optional inertia shaping, a PD version, an MPC version in development.
  • FZI cartesian_controllers
    Cartesian motion, force and compliance controllers for ros2_control (Humble, Iron, Jazzy), on joint position or velocity interfaces.
  • franka_ros2 Cartesian impedance example · libfranka
    Franka's reference Cartesian impedance controller for the FR3 (jazzy branch).
  • MoveIt Pro admittance (JTAC) · velocity/force control · MoveIt Servo
    MoveIt's compliance lives in MoveIt Pro: a joint-trajectory admittance controller and a hybrid velocity/force controller. MoveIt itself has no impedance controller; MoveIt Servo streams real-time targets.

The ros2_control ecosystem

  • joint_trajectory_controller
    The reference JTC: interpolates joint waypoints; robot_controllers' JTC follows its time-driven sampling and hold-at-end semantics.
  • JTC PR #1306: sample at t + dT
    Since Nov 2024 the JTC samples its command one control period ahead: the same idea as lookahead, for one period.
  • admittance_controller
    Chainable admittance from a tool F/T sensor; the JTC can feed it.
  • Controller chaining
    Cascaded controllers run in one cycle; our layout adds where each link of the chain physically runs.

Drivers and timing

  • moteus control modes
    Position, velocity and torque modes; mjbots advises putting as much of the law as possible in the drive's position controller.
  • moteus theory of operation
    The drive integrates its setpoint at the commanded velocity between frames: why the secant velocity matters.
  • Beckhoff TwinCAT: Dead time compensation
    Drive and encoder dead-time compensation in the motion stack.
  • Tomizuka 1987, ZPETC
    Using future reference samples to cancel tracking lag; lookahead is its pure-delay case.
  • Smith 1957, the Smith predictor
    Delay inside a feedback loop needs prediction (Chem. Eng. Prog. 53(5):217; explainer link).
