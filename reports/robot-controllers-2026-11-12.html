Findings

  1. Impact force is physics, not the controller. At 0.10 m/s every controller peaks at 28–35 N; at 0.20 m/s all of them fail the 60 N gate (67.9, 67.9, 66.9, 69.8, 70.5 N). The arm's own inertia hits the belt before any loop can react, as the Oct 26 impedance study found (a series spring is what cuts it). The controller's real lever is the dwell press: 17.8 N stiff vs 2.56 N impedance.
  2. Board impedance lags in free space (1.23° p95 vs 0.567° for the same law on the host). The board tracks a setpoint that reaches it 5.5 ms late over the bus. The host law compares the newest reference with a measurement 1.5 ms old, which adds a forward push. A cheap fix for every board-side controller is to sample the reference ahead by the measured bus delay.
  3. Soft springs and gear friction don't come back to the millimetre. Under the 15 N push the impedance variants deflect 63.2, 66.1, 67.1 mm and never return within 1 mm (the frictionloss deadband is ≈ 0.3 N·m / 40 N·m/rad). The stiff controller deflects 9 mm and recovers in 0.05 s.
  4. A late tick: the tape feeder slips, the time-driven controller skips. After the 0.5 s host stall the tape feeder ends 510 ms behind schedule; the joint trajectory controller ends 10.6 ms behind. The tape feeder also refuses 7 of 8 replans on R5.
  5. The robot_msgs sequence controller goes limp after a host stall. On S3.HostStall its ramp clock kept counting through the 0.5 s stall, so on resume it commanded a jump and its own speed guard aborted the sequence: “elbow was driven faster than 3.0 (+3.87, its drive pushing that way) in step 'move'”. This is the five-bar home button's controller, and the F09 bus hangs are exactly this case.
  6. Cost is small and similar: 40–100 µs CPU per tick for every controller (2 joints), far inside a 10 ms period. The board variant sends 5 fields per joint per tick, the host torque variant 3. Compute is thread CPU time, because this machine ran peer fleets at load ~100 and wall time counted their turns.

Decision surface

Done and proved
  • A new repo /home/bam/robot_controllers (local git), with the contract, 6 built controllers and 4 planned in the registry, the bench, metrics, W&B logging and the site. 229 package tests plus the site suite.
  • Full ladder run (53 episodes) → results/*/results.json (the ledger), mirrored to W&B, rendered on the site.
Decided for you (each one is a revert away)
  • A box is (input type, output type). W&B project names are Controller · <Input> to <Output>, plus a Controllers hub.
  • The plant is a planar 2-joint arm (MuJoCo) with a moteus-like board model checked tick-for-tick against bam_pid_tunning. It is not the five-bar.
  • Compute is scored as CPU time. A host-torque law gets "n/a" on the command-jump gate. The tape feeder is set to each rung's rate.
  • The recommendation rule: most rungs passed, then most gates passed, then the lowest compute. The leaderboard also shows the best per rung.
Left
  • Run the ladder on the real rack and on the five-bar twin. The Plant protocol is the seam: robotparts drivers implement read/write.
  • Cartesian impedance, admittance and OSC are planned in the registry (they need a kinematics model or a force signal).
  • Board-side reference lookahead (finding 2), and a stall-safe sequence controller (finding 5, robot_msgs).
Needs you
  • Public or not: the repo and site are local only. Recommendation: publish once you've looked at the site; a domain like robotcontrollers.com costs money, so it's yours.
  • A force signal on the rig (load-cell plate) before the contact rungs can run on hardware.
