Controllers v1: where feed-forward and feedback run, at what rate, and how far ahead

robot_controllers, contract amendments 3 and 4 · the planar MuJoCo twin and a UR5e on a moteus-like board model (4 kHz PD, 5.5 ms bus down, 1.5 ms up) · first sweep: 198 layouts × 7 rungs (8a77523ed6a0) · study: 794 episodes over rate, speed, lookahead, bus jitter, per-joint delays and a moving target (11a93931bb28) · sim only; nothing here touched the arm

Yes: v1 is feed-forward at plan time and feedback on the board, plus two free fixes on the host. Sample the plan one bus delay ahead (lookahead), and send the secant velocity (the straight line to the next sample) instead of the instantaneous one. At the rig's 50 Hz that takes the planar twin from 1.93° to 0.201° p95 on fast moves, and the UR5e from 1.64° to 0.349° (tool error 17.2 → 2.01 mm). Nothing extra runs on the host and nothing extra crosses the bus.

  • Why 50 Hz: the bus, not Python or physics. One fdcanusb takes ~15.5 ms to exchange with all seven boards, so a 10 ms tick can't hold it (2026-11-13, bam_ws PEP 0048). With the v1 recipe 50 Hz costs little: the planar twin is at 0.201° vs 0.122° at 100 Hz; the UR5e is at 0.349° vs 0.338°.
  • Feedback on the host can't run at 50 Hz: PD on the host is at 19.7° and computed torque on the host at 1.51° on the planar twin. Its loop carries one host period plus the 7 ms round trip; the board's carries 0.5 ms.
  • Lookahead calibrates from one run. One run with no lookahead gives the delay by lining the motion up with the plan. The tracking curve is a V with its bottom at the delay: best 5 ms on the planar twin and 6 ms on the UR5e (bus 5.5 ms); 1 ms off costs 0.109–0.196°. On a bus that serves joints in turn, calibrate per joint: on the UR5e that gave 0.227°, against 1.43° with one 5.5 ms lookahead.
  • Reliability: exact for a constant delay. With ±1 ms of bus jitter the v1 recipe is at 0.142° (planar, worst seed 0.176°); at ±3 ms, 0.283°. Still far better than no lookahead (1.68°).
  • Catching a moving item: on a belt at 0.25 m/s the tool runs 6.52 ms behind the item as built (1.81 mm p95) and 0.967 ms behind with v1 (0.462 mm). At 1 m/s: 6.22 mm, failing the 5 mm gate, against 0.591 mm.
  • Speed: v1 passes every speed tried, up to 656°/s on the planar twin and 460°/s on the UR5e (2.5× its rated joint speed). There it tracks 0.25° and 0.39°, and the boards start touching their torque caps (3.67 % and 3.95 % of the time).
  • On the UR5e the floor is friction. v1 sits at ~0.34° at every rate because the trajectory's torque column carries no dry friction. The dispatcher's check, not in the ledger: a Coulomb term halves U1's tool error (1.40 → 0.73 mm) and cuts the low-frequency feedback share from 16 % to 5 %, but it over-compensates wrist_3 at reversals.

Sim only: the planar MuJoCo twin and a UR5e (Menagerie links, bam_pid_tunning's friction and rotor values) on the same moteus-like board model and bus. 794 episodes in results/study/results.json.

Why 50 Hz, and how the rate changes things

50 Hz is the rate the real pick cycle runs at, and it was set by the bus. The 2026-11-13 wrist run asked for 100 Hz and was refused: four boards took 9.3 ms p95 per exchange on one fdcanusb, and all seven take ~15.5 ms (p95 16.4 ms, 2026-11-18). A 10 ms tick can't hold that, so the tick became 20 ms (bam_ws docs/peps/0048-full-arm-td4-pans-j1.md, full_arm.py:77-80). A second adapter is the way up if it's ever needed; nobody has measured it. One more caveat: the twin's 5.5 ms down-delay is a fitted number, not a timed one.

The board closes its PD at 4 kHz whatever the host does, so a board-side law only needs the host to carry the reference. Between two commands the board walks its setpoint forward at the velocity it was sent. Send the instantaneous velocity and the setpoint drifts off a curving path within each tick; send the secant and it lands exactly on the next sample. That one change is what makes 50 Hz nearly as good as 100 Hz.

Lookahead: why it works, how far, and how to calibrate it

Why it works. The bus delay is outside the board's loop: it only delays the setpoint. The plan is known ahead, so the host sends the setpoint meant for "now + 5.5 ms" and it arrives on time. It also stops the model torque from arriving late, which is why the as-built JTC (1.68°) barely beats no feed-forward at all. For feedback on the host the delay is inside the loop, so advancing the reference only compares it with an even staler measurement. That needs state prediction (a Smith predictor), or the loop moved into the drive.

How far. The bottom of the V is at the delay. With the secant velocity the curve is flatter and its bottom holds at 50 Hz too.

Calibrating it per joint. On one bus the boards are served in turn, so each joint's command lands at a different time. The study gave each joint a delay of 1.5 + 2.2·j ms (a one-adapter bus at ~2.2 ms per board) and tried four lookaheads. "Calibrated" is the one-run procedure: run once with no lookahead, line each joint's motion up with its plan, and use those shifts.

How reliable: a jittery bus

A fixed lookahead cancels the average delay; jitter leaves roughly speed × jitter. Industrial buses remove the jitter instead (EtherCAT latches every setpoint on a shared clock edge). On the twin the v1 recipe degrades gently:

The UR5e line is flat because its worst joint is a wrist held back by dry friction (~0.34°), which hides the jitter. The jitter is drawn per command and rounded to whole 0.25 ms board ticks, so a seed replays exactly (the builder's choice; jitter under a tick has no effect).

Catching a moving item

Timing error is position error: mm = belt speed × ms. Two delays have to be cancelled. One is on the command path, which is this lookahead. The other is on the perception path, from camera timestamp to plan, where the item is predicted forward from a timestamped detection. Both need one shared clock. For scale, ABB's conveyor tracking specifies ±2 mm at 150 mm/s.

The first sweep: every layout of every model (amendment 3)

Forward the trajectory, leave the feedback on the board, compute the model at plan time and sample the reference 5.5 ms ahead (the bus delay). That's your "first step" JTC plus one parameter, and it was the best layout of the 198: 0.347° p95 on the fast moves, against 1.68° without the lookahead, with no extra compute and no extra bus traffic.

  • Feed-forward: plan time. It equals host feed-forward at the reference and costs nothing per tick. The model cuts the feedback's share of the torque from 99.8 % to 12.1 % (below 50 Hz: 5.31 %). Without one, nothing passes: the arm sags and never settles after a host stall.
  • Feedback: on the board. Lookahead fixes every board-side law (about 1.7° → 0.35–0.5°) and hurts every host-side one, which already acts on stale data. A PD on the host at 100 Hz fails every rung. Computed torque on the host tracks well at 100 Hz (0.648°) but saturates at 50 Hz, which is the rate the real arm's pick cycle runs.
  • An unmodelled 0.3 kg payload shows up as low-frequency feedback (below-50 Hz share 5.31 % → 17.1 %), while tracking stays at 0.423°. That's the number that tells a feed-forward agent its model is missing mass.

118 of 198 layouts pass every gate on every rung. The ranking rule: cheapest layout within 10 % of the best passing tracking (the ledger's own summary; reasons per pair below).

The question, made precise

A controller here is a trajectory + a feed-forward model ff(q, q̇, q̈) → τ + a feedback model fb(q_ref, q̇_ref, q, q̇) → τ + a layout: where each model is evaluated (plan time, the host, the board, or split), at what rate, on which state (reference or measured), and how far ahead the reference is sampled. Both models are pure functions (robot_controllers.models); one composed controller (controllers/layout.py) places them, and it is tick-for-tick identical to the built JTC and the three joint-impedance controllers when placed the way they are (acceptance tests, written before the code).

Lookahead is the lever the board-side laws were missing

A board closes its PD on a setpoint that left the host 5.5 ms earlier. Sampling the trajectory 5.5 ms ahead lands each setpoint on the board when it is due. A host-side law already measured the arm a bus delay ago, so lookahead only makes it lead further.

The feed-forward ladder

Zach's KPI from Nov 11 ("low feedback torque, where most of it is coming from feedforward terms"), now fb_share_pct, in traj_compare's formula summed over the joints. With the rigid-body model about 7 of its 12 points sit above 50 Hz: the board's kd times its encoder quantisation (the "kd buzz"), which no model removes. The below-50 Hz share is what a better model could still take off the feedback.
